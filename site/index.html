What you're looking at

1990–2026 photos are real. Full Landsat true-colour scenes covering all of greater Hyderabad (USGS/NASA, rendered by Microsoft Planetary Computer): Landsat 5 · 9 Feb 1990, Landsat 7 · 29 Feb 2000, Landsat 5 · 31 Jan 2010, Landsat 8 · 27 Jan 2020, Landsat 9 · 20 Feb 2026. Each pixel is 30 m, so you see the city spreading, lakes and roads, not single houses. Between photo years the slider cross-fades two photos.

3D bars (zoomed out) cover the whole city: one bar per ~0.5 km cell, height = share of the cell that is built up in the slider year (GHSL, EU JRC; exaggerated), colour = decade it first became built up.

3D city (v3, zoom in past the bars): about 934,000 real building footprints across the core city (HITEC City, Gachibowli, Financial District, Banjara/Jubilee Hills, old city, Hussain Sagar, Secunderabad) from GlobalBuildingAtlas (TU Munich; Google + OSM footprints). Heights: OpenStreetMap height tags where mapped (~1,300 buildings), otherwise the building's tallest point in Google Open Buildings 2.5D Temporal (2023, ~835,000 buildings), otherwise GlobalBuildingAtlas's estimate. Satellite-derived heights are estimates: expect a few metres of error, and the newest towers (after mid-2023) may be missing or short. When a building appears on the slider comes from Google's 2016/2019/2023 layers and, before 2016, from its neighbourhood's GHSL growth curve, not its true construction date. Windows, colours, rooftop tanks and lit windows are drawn by the app, not photographed. Charminar and the Buddha statue are hand-built models; the Metro viaduct is drawn from the Metro line.

Zoomed in at 2020+ the detailed photo is today's Esri World Imagery (Maxar, Earthstar Geographics).

2040 is a prediction, not fact. Built-up area from GHSL (EU JRC); growth placed by distance to Metro (incl. announced Phase 2), ORR, RRR, airport and Future City. Phase 2 lines and the RRR south section are approximate.
